# Did 2020 make companies rewrite?

Milestone 4: a test of whether companies that wrote their annual report after COVID-19 arrived rewrote their risk sections, or added to them. Every number here is in `results/test2020.json`, written by:

```
uv run python -m pipeline.run test2020
```

The sample, the exposure, the outcomes and the comparison years were fixed in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-11-m4-test-2020.md` before any outcome was looked at.

## The answer

**They added a little, and rewrote nothing that this design can detect.**

Companies that filed on or after 11 March 2020 added sentences about COVID amounting to 1.3% of their risk section; those that filed before added 0.2%. Their other new text, the sentences they removed and the sentences they edited in place did not change relative to earlier years. The larger change came a year later, and it looks like the SEC's new rules more than the pandemic.

## The design

Reports on fiscal 2019 were filed across the weeks in which the pandemic began. A company that filed on 2 March 2020 wrote its risk section before the WHO declared a pandemic; one that filed on 25 March wrote it after.

That is not an experiment: companies that file late differ from those that file early. But they differ every year, and in earlier years nothing happened in mid-March. So the earlier years measure the standing gap between late and early filers, and the estimate is how much that gap changed in 2020 (a difference-in-differences), with each company compared with itself in other years.

- **Sample:** filings for fiscal 2016–2019 with a previous-year filing (12,648), a fiscal year ending in December (9,846), the same year end as the year before (9,704), and one matching 10-K so the filing date is certain (9,692 filings, 4,163 companies).
- **Exposure:** filed on or after 11 March of the following year. The share is the same every year: 39.4%, 38.3%, 39.5%, and 38.7% for fiscal 2019.
- **Outcomes:** the share of sentences that are new, the share of last year's sentences removed, the share edited in place (near), the growth of the section, and the share that are new and don't mention COVID.
- **Model:** outcome on exposure and exposure × fiscal 2019, with company and year fixed effects and standard errors clustered by company.

## Did filing late mean writing about COVID?

Mostly, but the line is not sharp, and that limits what the design can see.

| Fiscal 2019 reports | Filings | Mention COVID | COVID sentences added, share of section |
|---|---|---|---|
| Filed before 11 March 2020 | 1,464 | 39.1% | 0.2% |
| Filed on or after | 925 | 82.1% | 1.3% |

For fiscal 2018 the same two groups mention it in 0.1% and 0.2% of filings, as they should. Week by week, the share of fiscal 2019 reports mentioning COVID climbs from about 28% three weeks before the cutoff to 45%, 58%, 75% and then 88% two weeks after. By the cutoff date four in ten "unexposed" filers had already written something. The comparison is between more and less exposed, not exposed and unexposed, which shrinks whatever effect there is.

## Results

Late filers, fiscal 2019, relative to their own other years and to early filers. Shares are in percentage points of the section.

| Outcome | Early filers: 2016–18 → 2019 | Late filers: 2016–18 → 2019 | Estimate | 95% interval |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New sentences | 9.9 → 9.9 | 11.1 → 13.0 | +0.8 | −0.6 to +2.3 |
| Removed | 7.3 → 7.4 | 8.6 → 8.9 | −0.6 | −2.1 to +0.8 |
| Edited in place | 11.7 → 11.8 | 8.4 → 7.9 | −0.5 | −1.2 to +0.2 |
| New, not mentioning COVID | 9.9 → 9.7 | 11.1 → 11.7 | −0.4 | −1.8 to +1.1 |
| Growth of the section (log points) | 0.030 → 0.027 | 0.036 → 0.049 | +0.010 | −0.015 to +0.035 |

Read plainly:

- **Late filers' new text rose by about 2 points in the raw averages, and most of that is COVID sentences.** Take those out and the rise is 0.6 points raw, and nothing once each company is compared with itself.
- **Removals did not rise.** Whatever late filers added, they did not make room for it.
- **None of the five estimates can be told from zero.** The interval for new sentences runs from a small decrease to an increase of about two points. A change as large as fiscal 2020's (below) is ruled out; a small one is not.

That pattern is appending. It is also small: 1.3% of a typical section is three or four sentences.

### Checks

**The same test in years with no shock.** Moving the "event" to fiscal 2017 or 2018 gives +0.3 and +0.4 points for new sentences (intervals −1.2 to +1.8 and −1.2 to +2.0) and −0.2 and −0.05 for removals. Of the ten placebo estimates across the five outcomes, one has an interval that excludes zero (edited in place, fiscal 2018: −0.8, −1.6 to −0.1), about what chance gives.

**By filing week.** Relative to the busiest week before the cutoff, fiscal 2019's new sentences are +1.2 points in the cutoff week and +1.8 two weeks later, but the weeks before the cutoff show +1.2 and +1.7 as well, and every interval includes zero. There is no step at the cutoff. For new text not mentioning COVID the estimates after the cutoff are between −1.5 and +0.8.

**One thing changed at a time** (new sentences; main estimate +0.8):

| Variant | Estimate | 95% interval | Filings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exposure set by the *previous* year's filing date | +0.4 | −1.1 to +1.8 | 9,692 |
| No company fixed effects | +1.9 | +0.6 to +3.2 | 9,692 |
| With filings that have several candidate 10-Ks | +0.8 | −0.6 to +2.3 | 9,704 |
| With companies whose fiscal year end changed | +0.8 | −0.7 to +2.3 | 9,834 |
| Only filings within three weeks of the cutoff | +1.0 | −0.5 to +2.5 | 7,808 |

Two rows matter. Without company fixed effects the estimate is larger and its interval excludes zero: the late-filing group of 2020 added more than late-filing groups of other years. With them it halves: the same companies did not add much more than they usually do. The difference says the group changed. The SEC let companies delay their filings in spring 2020, and 5.7% of December filers filed after 1 May that year against about 2% in the three years before (measured before the analysis; see the plan). Who took the extension was not random. Setting exposure by the previous year's date, which no one could choose in response to the pandemic, gives the smallest estimate of all.

For removals every variant is between −0.7 and +0.2, with intervals that include zero.

## The year after

Reports on fiscal 2020 were written a year into the pandemic and, for 86.4% of them, after the SEC's amended risk-factor rules took effect on 9 November 2020. This data can't separate the two, so what follows is description.

| All paired filings with one matching 10-K | Fiscal 2018 | Fiscal 2019 | Fiscal 2020 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Filings | 3,126 | 3,032 | 2,967 |
| New sentences | 10.5% | 10.8% | 14.8% |
| ...of which mention COVID | 0.0% | 0.5% | 2.5% |
| Removed | 7.7% | 7.9% | 10.8% |
| Section has a risk-factor summary heading | 0.1% | 0.1% | 15.3% |
| Section has a "General risk factors" heading | 1.5% | 1.2% | 22.1% |

Fiscal 2020 is where sections changed: new text up four points, removals up three. Only 2.5 of the 14.8 points of new text mention COVID.

The two headings are what the new rules ask for (a summary when the section is long, and generic risks gathered under a general caption), and they go from almost absent to 15% and 22% of sections in one year.

A split inside fiscal 2020 points the same way. The 404 reports filed before the rules took effect (fiscal years ending early in 2020) added 14.2% and **removed 6.0%**, less than a normal year. The 2,563 filed after added 14.9% and **removed 11.6%**. Pandemic without the rules: add, don't cut. Pandemic with the rules: add and cut. The two groups differ in fiscal year end and in how far into the pandemic they wrote, so this is suggestive and no more.

**What was added for fiscal 2019 mostly stayed.** Of the COVID sentences added then that could be checked a year later, 72.5% were still there (3,730 sentences). For that year's other additions it was 76.9%, and for additions in 2016–2018, 79.3%.

## Limits

- **Filing date is not assigned at random**, and in 2020 some companies chose theirs. The fixed effects, the placebo years and the previous-year exposure each address part of that; none removes it.
- **The contrast is diluted.** 39% of the comparison group had already written about COVID.
- **One event, one year.** The intervals are what one cross-section of about 2,400 companies can give.
- **The outcomes inherit the measurement's error** (`docs/measurement.md`). Near matches are right about 95% of the time and new sentences 99.6%; a sentence kept with a COVID clause inserted counts as edited in place, not as new, which is why that outcome is reported too.
- **"Mentions COVID" is a word match** (`covid`, `coronavirus`, `sars-cov`, `2019-ncov`). A new sentence about remote work or supply chains that doesn't use one of those words is counted as not about COVID.
