The Challenge
Understanding SEC Risk Disclosures

SEC 10-K filings contain critical risk information, but manually analyzing thousands of disclosures is impractical. This project automates the classification and analysis of risk factors, enabling scalable insights into corporate risk communication patterns.

02
The Open Question
How Much Is Boilerplate?

How much of what companies say about risk is copied, from peers or from their own earlier filings? This first version compared two similarity scores, but they measure different amounts of text, so the comparison doesn't answer it. The rework measures copying directly, sentence by sentence.

Model Performance Comparison (F1 Score)
Read With Caution

These F1 scores overstate real performance. Minority classes were upsampled before the train/test split, so copies of test rows were also in training; most test rows share a company with training rows; and the ensemble was chosen on test F1. The labels come from keyword rules. So the ensemble's lead over DistilBERT (71.2% vs 57.8%) doesn't hold.

Semantic Analysis

Two Similarity Scores

What each one actually measured

TF-IDF (Lexical)

21.3%

Mean TF-IDF cosine between companies, over up to 50,000 characters of each section, with rare words weighted up.

SBERT (Semantic)

51.0%

Mean SBERT cosine between companies, over only about the first 1,400 characters of each section (the model's 256-token limit).

Why These Can't Be Compared

The two scores read different amounts of text on different scales, so the gap between them doesn't show that companies say the same things in different words. A check confirms it: shuffling each section's words, which destroys their meaning, raises the SBERT mean from 51.0% to 57.9%. The rework measures copying directly instead.
