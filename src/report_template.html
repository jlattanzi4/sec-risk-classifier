How much of a risk disclosure is boilerplate?

Every public company lists the risks it faces in its annual report, and most of that list is last year's list. This project compared %%filings%% risk sections with the same company's section from the year before, sentence by sentence. The median section keeps %%median_exact%% of its sentences word for word, and %%median_new%% of it is new.

What the study found

  • %%pooled_exact%% of sentences are last year's, word for word. Another %%pooled_near%% are light edits of one, and %%pooled_new%% are new.
  • The labels hold up on real filings, and where they don't is known. On pairs that two judges agree on, an “edited” match is right %%near_right%% of the time and a “new” call %%new_right%%; the stricter judge alone puts “edited” at %%judge_one_near%%. The blind spot: a sentence kept with its claim reversed still counts as carried over.
  • Boilerplate travels between companies. %%peer_full%% of full sentences are filed word for word by at least %%peer_k%% other companies in the same year. That share rose from %%peer_first%% to %%peer_last%% over the period.
  • A new sentence has a %%gone_first_year%% chance of being gone the next year. Sentences shared with other companies last a median of %%median_shared%% filings, against %%median_own%% for a company's own, and %%var_within%% of the variation in rewriting is a company differing from itself year to year.
  • The pandemic made companies add, not rewrite. Companies that filed after it was declared added a few sentences about COVID. The change in their new text overall, %%did_new%% points (95% interval %%did_new_span%%), can't be told from zero. The larger change came a year later, with the SEC's new risk-factor rules.

What a typical section is made of

The comparison covers %%sentences%% sentences from %%companies%% companies, in reports on fiscal %%year_first%% to %%year_last%%. Each sentence gets one label, by comparing it with every sentence in the same company's section a year earlier.

  • Kept word for word: %%pooled_exact%%. Digits and capitals are ignored, so a sentence that only updates a figure or a date counts as kept.
  • Edited: %%pooled_near%%. It shares at least %%tau_v_percent%% of its five-word runs with one of last year's sentences.
  • Reworded: %%pooled_para%%. No shared phrasing, but a sentence-embedding model places it close to one of last year's.
  • New: %%pooled_new%%. None of the above.

Going the other way, %%pooled_removed%% of last year's sentences have no match this year: they were cut.

The totals hide a wide spread. In the median filing %%median_new%% of the sentences are new, but a tenth of filings are under %%new_p10%% and a tenth are over %%new_p90%%. %%nothing_new%% filings added nothing at all.

Can the labels be trusted?

No person labeled anything in this project. The labels were checked three ways instead: on pairs built so the right answer is known, on real pairs read by two language-model judges, and on text where there is nothing to find.

Pairs whose answer is known

Real sentences were each given four partners: a light edit, a paraphrase, a version with its claim reversed, and an unrelated sentence. One language model wrote them and a second confirmed them. On the half held out for testing, a “carried over” call was right %%known_precision%% of the time and found %%known_recall%% of the pairs it should have.

That figure leaves the reversed claims out, and they are the method's blind spot. Of %%hard_n%% sentences whose claim was reversed by changing a word or two, %%hard_called%% were called carried over. The matcher follows words. So does the embedding model behind the “reworded” label: given a sentence and the same words in random order, it calls them a match %%shuffled_clear%% of the time.

Real pairs, two judges

How often do real filings contain such edits? %%judged_n%% pairs of real sentences were labeled by one Claude model, and %%judge_two_n%% of them again by a second that saw no scores. The two agree only moderately: a kappa of %%kappa_first%% under the first instructions and %%kappa_second%% once the instructions spelled out the cases they split on, still under the bar of %%kappa_bar%% set in advance. By the rule fixed beforehand, the check uses only the %%agreed_n%% pairs both judges agree on.

Constructed pairs set the cut-off too low

Tuned on the constructed pairs, the cut-off for “reworded” comes out at a cosine of %%tau_p_constructed%%. Real filings show that is far too low: %%constructed_cut_wrong%% of what it calls reworded was judged to be a different sentence. A constructed paraphrase is compared with one unrelated sentence. A real sentence is compared with every unmatched sentence in last year's filing, which is about the same business in the same vocabulary, and the closest of dozens clears a low bar easily. The cut-off used here is tuned on the judged real pairs instead: %%tau_p%%.

How long a sentence lasts

Following each added sentence through the same company's later filings gives it a life. That can be done for %%chains%% sentences whose arrival was seen. The %%first_filing_millions%% million sentences already in a company's first filing are left out, because their age is unknown.

A new sentence has a %%gone_first_year%% chance of being gone the next year, and the risk falls every year it survives. Half are gone within %%median_all%% filings; %%survive_last%% are still there after %%last_age%%.

Sentences that other companies also file last longer. A sentence filed by at least %%peer_k%% other companies the year it was added has a median life of %%median_shared%% filings, against %%median_own%% for a sentence of the company's own.

A model that holds fixed where a sentence sits, how long its section is, when it was added and which company wrote it says the same, and says when. A shared sentence is %%less_first%% less likely to be cut at its first filing and %%less_later%% less at its second and third. After that it is no different from any other sentence (hazard ratio %%hr_shared_old%%, 95% interval %%hr_shared_old_span%%). Boilerplate is protected while it is new.

Rewriting is an event, not a trait. Of the variation in how much of a filing is new, %%var_company%% belongs to the company, %%var_year%% to the year and %%var_industry%% to the industry. The other %%var_within%% is the same company differing from itself from one year to the next.

Did %%after_year%% make companies rewrite?

The pandemic gives a test. Reports on fiscal %%event_year%% were filed across the weeks in which it began. A company that filed in early March wrote its risk section before the WHO declared a pandemic on 11 March; one that filed at the end of the month wrote it after. Late filers differ from early filers every year, so the comparison is how much that gap changed, with each company compared with itself in the three years before (%%did_filings%% filings from %%did_companies%% companies). The design was fixed before any outcome was looked at.

They added a little, and rewrote nothing this design can detect. Late filers added sentences about COVID worth %%late_covid%% of the section, against %%early_covid%% for early filers. Relative to their own earlier years, their new sentences changed by %%did_new%% points (95% interval %%did_new_span%%) and their removals by %%did_removed%% (%%did_removed_span%%).

Three cautions. The contrast is diluted: %%early_mention%% of the early filers had already mentioned COVID. Without comparing each company with itself, the estimate is %%no_fixed_new%% and its interval excludes zero (%%no_fixed_span%%), which says the group of late filers changed more than their writing did: the SEC let companies file late that spring, and who did was not random. Setting exposure by the previous year's filing date, which nobody could choose in response, gives %%usual_new%%. And week by week there is no step at the cut-off.

The larger change came a year later, and it points at the SEC. Averaged across filings, the unit of this test, reports on fiscal %%after_year%% are %%after_new%% new sentences (%%after_prev_new%% the year before) and cut %%after_removed%% of the previous year's (%%after_prev_removed%%). Only %%after_new_covid%% of the section is new text that mentions COVID.

%%after_rules_share%% of those reports were filed after the SEC's amended risk-factor rules took effect in November %%after_year%%. The rules ask for a summary when the section is long and for generic risks to be gathered under a general heading, and a summary heading appears in %%summary_heading%% of fiscal %%after_year%% sections, up from %%summary_heading_before%%. The %%rules_before_n%% reports filed before the rules cut %%rules_before_removed%% of last year's sentences; the %%rules_after_n%% filed after cut %%rules_after_removed%%. The two groups differ in other ways, so this is description and not a test.

Limits, and how to check this

  • No person labeled anything. Both judges are language models from one family and may share blind spots. Where they disagree, the write-up shows both.
  • An edit that changes a claim counts as carried over. A sentence kept with one word reversed is “edited”.
  • Only the previous year is compared. A sentence dropped one year and restored the next is new when it returns.
  • The %%after_year%% test is one event, and the date a company files is not assigned at random.
