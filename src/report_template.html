Under review

A review in November 2026 found that the first version's two headline findings don't hold up. They're struck below, with the reason in their place.

  • Filings are 51% alike in meaning but only 21% alike in wording. The two similarity scores read different amounts of text, so they can't be compared.
  • A TF-IDF ensemble classifies risks with %%f1%% F1. That score comes from an evaluation where copies of test rows were also in the training set.

What comes next

The rework splits each section into sentences and asks a narrower question: how much of a company's risk section is copied, from its peers or from its own previous filing?

It will report shares rather than averages: the sentences with a near-verbatim match elsewhere, the ones that are paraphrased, and how much carries over from year to year, with 2020's pandemic disclosures as a natural test. Every number will come with a baseline, like shuffled text and random pairs of filings, so it's clear what counts as high.
