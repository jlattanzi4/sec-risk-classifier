# Milestone 3: Explain Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Say how long a risk sentence lasts once a company adds it, what makes one last longer, and how much of a filing's rewriting belongs to the company, its industry and the year.

**Architecture:** Milestone 2's labels link each sentence to its match in the next filing. Following those links year by year turns every sentence a company *adds* into a chain with a length and an ending (removed, or still there when the data stops). Survival curves come straight from counts of chains at risk and removed at each age. The hazard model and the variance decomposition are hierarchical Bayesian models fit with NUTS (numpyro), so every estimate comes with an interval and the fits are checked with the usual diagnostics.

**Tech Stack:** pandas, numpy; lifelines (Cox model and the proportional-hazards test); numpyro on JAX (hierarchical models); arviz-style diagnostics computed with numpyro's own utilities.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-09-copying-rework-design.md` (Layer 2). Inputs: `data/interim/labels/`, `data/interim/peers/`, `data/interim/filings.parquet`, `data/interim/filing_metrics.parquet` (milestone 2).

## Global Constraints

- Only consecutive years link. A missing year ends a chain as censored, never as removed.
- A chain is counted only if its start was seen: the sentence is labeled `new` in a filing whose previous-year filing exists. Sentences already present in a company's first filing have an unknown age and are left out of survival (their number is reported).
- Time is counted in filings. A sentence that appears in one filing and is gone from the next has duration 1.
- `removed` means what milestone 2 means by it: no sentence in the next filing matches it exactly, nearly, or as a rewording.
- Peer-shared is fixed at birth: the sentence's normalized form is filed by at least 5 other groups of companies in the year it first appears (milestone 2's primary definition).
- Random choices use seed 0. Every number in `docs/explain.md` comes from `results/explain.json`.
- Decision rules fixed here: the proportional-hazards check and what follows from it (Task 3), the convergence standard for the Bayesian fits (Task 4), and the primary outcome scale for the variance decomposition (Task 5).

## Decisions this plan makes

- **A chain follows the previous filing's side of each pair.** If last year's sentence is matched this year, the chain continues at the sentence that best matches it; if it is `removed`, the chain ends. Two of last year's sentences may continue into the same sentence (a merge); both chains go on.
- **One chain per distinct new sentence.** A sentence added twice in the same filing starts one chain.
- **Discrete time.** Filings are annual, so durations are whole numbers with massive ties. The spec asks for a Cox model with a proportional-hazards check and a discrete-time model if the check fails. Both are fit: the Cox model as the check, and a discrete-time hazard model (complementary log-log link, which is the grouped-time version of proportional hazards) with a company random effect as the model that is reported.
- **Company frailty by sampling companies.** The hazard model is fit on a random sample of 2,000 companies, stratified by SIC division, with data collapsed to counts per company and covariate cell. The write-up says so.
- **Variance decomposition on the share itself.** The primary model is linear in `share_new`, so the variance shares read directly as shares of the variation in rewriting. A second fit on the logit of the share is the robustness check.

## Review Focus

1. A company with a gap year: chains alive before the gap are censored at the gap, and sentences in the first filing after it start no chains. (Task 1)
2. A sentence born in the last corpus year, or in a company's last filing: duration 1, censored, never at risk. (Task 1)
3. Two previous sentences matched to the same current sentence: both chains continue, and neither is dropped or double-ended. (Task 1)
4. An age at which nothing is at risk (the oldest ages in a small subgroup): the survival curve stops there instead of dividing by zero. (Task 2)
5. A sampler that has not converged (R-hat over 1.01 or too few effective samples): the stage fails loudly instead of writing intervals. (Task 4)

### Task 2: Survival curves

**Files:** Create `pipeline/survival.py`, `tests/test_survival.py`.

**Interfaces:**
- `life_table(chains: pd.DataFrame, by: str | None = None) -> pd.DataFrame`: per group and age: `at_risk`, `removed`, `hazard`, `survival` (Kaplan–Meier), and Greenwood 95% bounds on the log-log scale. A chain is at risk at age *t* if its duration is at least *t* and it is not censored at exactly *t*.
- `median_survival(table) -> float | None`: the first age at which survival is at or below 0.5; None if it never is.

- [ ] **Step 1: Failing tests.** Ten chains with a hand-computed table (durations and events chosen so the hazards are 0.2, 0.25, 0.5); censoring at an age removes the chain from that age's risk set; a group with nobody at risk at an age has no row for it; survival never rises; the bounds contain the estimate.

### Task 3: The proportional-hazards check

**Files:** Create `pipeline/hazard.py`, `tests/test_hazard.py`.

**Interfaces:**
- `company_sample(chains, n=2000, seed=0) -> pd.DataFrame`: chains of a random sample of companies, the same share from each SIC division.
- `cox_check(sample) -> dict`: a Cox model (lifelines, Efron ties, standard errors clustered by company) of removal on `peer_shared`, log `n_words`, `position`, log `section_sentences`, birth year (centered) and SIC division, with the Schoenfeld-residual test of proportional hazards per covariate and overall.

**Rule (fixed now):** proportional hazards is taken to fail if the test rejects at 0.01 for `peer_shared` or for the model as a whole. Either way the discrete-time model in Task 4 is the one reported, because annual data are grouped; if the check fails, that model lets the effect of `peer_shared` differ by age band (1, 2–3, 4+), and the write-up shows the three effects.

- [ ] **Step 1: Failing tests.** On simulated chains where one covariate doubles the hazard at every age, the fitted hazard ratio is within 10% of 2 and the test does not reject; where the covariate only matters at age 1, the test rejects.
- [ ] **Step 2–5:** run, implement, run, commit "Check proportional hazards with a Cox model".

### Task 4: The hazard model with company frailty

**Files:** Modify `pipeline/hazard.py`, `tests/test_hazard.py`.

**Interfaces:**
- `person_periods(sample) -> pd.DataFrame`: collapsed counts `at_risk`, `removed` per company, age (1 to 8, with 8 meaning 8 or more), and covariate cell (`peer_shared`; `n_words` in thirds; `position` in thirds; `section_sentences` in thirds; birth year in four bands; SIC division).
- `fit_hazard(periods, vary_by_age: bool, seed=0) -> dict`: complementary log-log hazard with an intercept per age, the covariates above, and a normal random intercept per company, fit with NUTS (4 chains, 1,000 warm-up, 1,000 draws). Returns hazard ratios with 95% credible intervals, the company standard deviation, and diagnostics.

**Convergence standard (fixed now):** every reported parameter has R-hat ≤ 1.01 and at least 400 effective draws, and there are no divergent transitions. Otherwise the function raises.

- [ ] **Step 1: Failing tests.** On simulated person-periods with a known hazard ratio of 0.5 and a company standard deviation of 0.6, the intervals contain both; counts collapse correctly (a chain with duration 3 and an event contributes at-risk 1, 1, 1 and removed 0, 0, 1; censored at 3 contributes at-risk 1, 1 only... see Global Constraints); a fit forced to 20 draws raises on the convergence standard.
- [ ] **Step 2–5:** run, implement, run on the real sample, commit "Fit the hazard of removal with a company random effect".

### Task 5: What drives rewriting

**Files:** Create `pipeline/variance.py`, `tests/test_variance.py`.

**Interfaces:**
- `fit_variance(metrics: pd.DataFrame, outcome="share_new", scale="share", seed=0) -> dict`: `outcome ~ 1 + company + division + year` with normal random intercepts and normal residuals, fit with NUTS. Returns each component's standard deviation and its share of the total variance, with 95% credible intervals, and diagnostics. `scale="logit"` fits the logit of the share (shares clipped to [0.005, 0.995]).

- [ ] **Step 1: Failing tests.** On simulated data with known standard deviations (company 0.10, year 0.03, division 0.02, residual 0.08), each interval contains the truth and the variance shares sum to 1.
